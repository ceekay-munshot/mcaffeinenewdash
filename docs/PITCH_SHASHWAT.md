## What one of them actually looks like

Take **Continuity Printers**, a packaging vendor.

Their accounts show they borrow at **13.99%**. That's expensive money. So when
your buyer offers to pay them in 15 days instead of 60, that early cash is
worth roughly **1.17% a month** to them — and you can ask for that as a
discount, priced off their own cost of borrowing. Not a guess. Their number.

Three more, same idea:

- **ValueTree** is the healthiest vendor we track (68/100) and earns well above
  its cost of capital. They can afford to give margin — push on **price**.
- **Arovea** is in the distress zone. Do **not** squeeze them; qualify a second
  source before you have to.
- **EPL** just moved from Blackstone to Indorama and holds a AA credit rating.
  They can hold their price. Push on **volume and service**, not rate.

That's the whole point: the same question — "where do I push?" — gets a
different answer per vendor, and the answer comes with its evidence attached.

## What it can't tell you yet

Worth being straight about this.

1. **It reads accounts, not conversations.** It knows a vendor *could* give
   margin. It doesn't know whether your buyer already tried.
2. **Filed accounts run a year behind.** They're audited and reliable, but
   they're not last month.
3. **It says nothing about quality.** Right now a vendor who is cheap and
   financially strong looks great — even if their reject rate is poor.
4. **Price benchmarks are open-market research**, not a paid price feed. Good
   enough to frame a negotiation, not to settle one.

## What we'd build next

**Step 1 — know each vendor. Done.**
Their own numbers, their own risks, one page each.

**Step 2 — compare them against each other.**
Today the tool ranks vendors by financial health. It should also answer: for
*this* ingredient, who else could credibly supply it, and are they stronger or
weaker than who we use now? We've started this — the piece that's missing is a
reliable list of who actually sells what in India.

**Step 3 — put a number on the deal.**
Your rate against the market and against every other quote, costed against
annual volume. The working version is already in the dashboard: on six sample
materials it flagged **₹32 lakh a year** of gap. That number is only as good as
the quotes and the volumes we feed it — which is why we need yours.

---

## Two things we need from you

**1. Quality data.** A quality test report or an incoming-inspection spec for a
few materials. Without it the tool will keep recommending the cheapest healthy
vendor, and a buyer who knows that vendor's reject rate will stop trusting it.
This is the single biggest gap.

**2. A decision on paid data.** Some of Step 2 and Step 3 needs sources we
don't have — supplier directories, import/export records, price feeds. We'll
bring you a costed list with what each one adds. We're not asking for budget
today; we're asking you to tell us which questions are worth paying to answer.
